Open SOC SIEM — Browser-based investigation labs for SOC and AI security training.

Investigate sample or real logs in the browser and see every finding explained in plain language. Instructors can turn their own logs into graded classroom labs. Nothing to install, no accounts, and log files never leave the computer.

Two ways to start

Learners can begin an investigation in one click. Instructors can build a lab from their own logs without writing code.

For students and self-learners

Try a sample investigation

  1. Open the Log Analyzer with sample Linux, web server and AI agent logs already loaded.
  2. Follow the timeline, charts and IP table to the suspicious activity.
  3. Open each finding to read what happened, why it matters and what to check next.
  4. Turn on challenge mode to test yourself before the findings are revealed.
Try a sample investigation

For instructors

Build a classroom lab

  1. Load your own logs: Linux syslog or auth.log, Apache or Nginx, JSON lines, or CSV (including Windows events).
  2. Add custom detection rules for anything the built-in rules miss. No coding is needed.
  3. Write questions, or generate them from the findings and check the answers.
  4. Download one lab file and post it on your course page.
  5. Students open it, answer with instant feedback and download their answers to submit.
Build a classroom lab

How a classroom lab travels

The lab file carries the logs, your rules and your questions. Answers are stored scrambled, which discourages peeking; use labs for practice and coursework rather than high-stakes exams.

Built for the classroom

  • No installationRuns in any current browser, including Chromebooks and Apple Silicon laptops.
  • Logs stay localFiles are read in the browser and never uploaded.
  • Explained findingsEach finding says what happened, why it matters and what to check next.
  • Your own dataAny syslog, web, JSON-lines or CSV log can become a lab.

Open SOC SIEM is a teaching tool, not a production SIEM. Its detection rules are deliberately simple and readable. For real monitoring, students should move on to a full platform such as Wazuh or Security Onion.
